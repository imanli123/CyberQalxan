# PROJECT SUMMARY

The subject of this project is the development of **CyberQalxan**, a hybrid cybersecurity architecture that leverages blockchain technology to secure centralized Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems against insider threats and unauthorized data manipulation.

The primary purpose of CyberQalxan is to mathematically guarantee the immutability of digital audit trails without degrading the speed or storage capacity of existing enterprise networks. Currently, centralized log databases inherently trust administrators with root access. Statistics show that insider threats and Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) frequently exploit this by altering or deleting historical logs to erase evidence of a breach [1].

To solve this critical vulnerability, CyberQalxan utilizes an **"Off-Chain Merkle Batching"** methodology. Instead of attempting to store massive amounts of raw, sensitive enterprise data directly on a blockchain—which causes network bottlenecks and violates data privacy laws—our system acts as a zero-trust middleware. Using **Wazuh** as our primary prototype and testing environment, CyberQalxan batches system logs every 60 seconds and creates a single cryptographic fingerprint (a Merkle Root) of that data.

This unique fingerprint is then permanently anchored to a private, permissioned blockchain network. An automated auditor script sequentially cross-references the centralized Wazuh database against the immutable blockchain ledger. If a corrupt administrator or hacker alters even a single character in the historical logs, the system instantly detects the hash mismatch and flags the tampering. CyberQalxan successfully delivers Web3 cryptographic certainty while maintaining traditional Web2 processing speeds.

# ADDED VALUE AND INNOVATION

## 2.1. The Problem and Limitations of Existing Solutions

In modern enterprise networks, Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems are the ultimate source of truth for digital forensics. However, traditional SIEMs operate on a centralized architecture that inherently trusts users with administrative privileges. This creates a critical "Single Point of Failure" regarding data integrity. According to industry reports, insider threats and compromised credentials are among the most common and costly vectors for data breaches, often taking over 200 days to identify [1]. If a corrupt insider or an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) compromises the SIEM server, they can easily alter or delete historical logs to cover their tracks, rendering the audit trail legally and operationally useless.

Current attempts to solve this using blockchain technology generally fall into the "Pure On-Chain" trap. Startups have attempted to write raw enterprise logs directly to public or private blockchains. This approach fails in enterprise environments for three reasons:

- **Throughput Bottlenecks:** Public blockchains like Ethereum typically process 15 to 30 transactions per second (TPS) [2]. They mathematically cannot handle the thousands of events per second (EPS) generated by standard enterprise networks.
- **Data Privacy Violations:** Storing raw system or user data on an immutable ledger permanently violates data protection regulations. For example, under GDPR Article 17 (and equivalent KVKK laws), individuals possess the "Right to Erasure" [3]. Because data on a pure blockchain can never be deleted, the enterprise immediately fails legal compliance.
- **High Computational Costs:** Processing massive volumes of raw text through heavy consensus algorithms creates unsustainable infrastructure and cloud computing costs.

## 2.2. The Innovative Aspect and Originality of CyberQalxan

The proposed **CyberQalxan** architecture completely differentiates itself by decoupling *data storage* from *data verification*.

The originality lies in our **"Off-Chain Merkle Batching"** architecture. Instead of putting logs on the blockchain, CyberQalxan leaves the raw, sensitive data inside the high-speed, centralized SIEM (Wazuh). A Python Aggregator acts as a bridge, reading 60 seconds of logs, hashing them into a Merkle Tree, and anchoring only the 64-character Merkle Root to the Hyperledger Besu blockchain.

This creates unprecedented added value for businesses:

- **Web2 Speed with Web3 Security:** Institutions keep the lightning-fast search capabilities of their traditional databases while gaining the mathematical immutability of a blockchain.
- **Absolute Privacy Compliance:** Because the blockchain only stores cryptographic hashes (which cannot be reverse-engineered into the original logs), the system is 100% compliant with strict data privacy laws.
- **Zero-Friction Integration:** CyberQalxan requires no changes to how endpoint agents generate logs, allowing seamless integration into any existing network infrastructure via REST API.

## 2.4. SWOT Analysis

To evaluate the strategic positioning of CyberQalxan, the following SWOT analysis has been conducted:

- **Strengths:** Lightweight processing via the 1-transaction-per-minute limit; utilizes Hyperledger Besu's enterprise-grade QBFT consensus for absolute finality; requires zero endpoint reconfiguration.
- **Weaknesses:** Relies on the continuous uptime of the central SIEM's REST API to fetch logs; the 5-minute aggregation delay means real-time (sub-second) verification is not possible (though unnecessary for historical auditing).
- **Opportunities:** The rising regulatory demand for zero-trust architectures in the financial, defense, and government sectors; potential to package the Python Aggregator and Auditor scripts as a commercial SaaS plug-in for major SIEM providers.
- **Threats:** A highly coordinated 51% attack on the private validator nodes (mitigated by strict geographical and network isolation of the 5 VMs).

## 2.5. Feasibility Analysis

- **Technical Feasibility:** Extremely high. CyberQalxan leverages heavily vetted, open-source foundational technologies (Wazuh, Python hashlib/web3.py, and Hyperledger Besu) rather than attempting to write untested cryptographic algorithms from scratch.
- **Economic Feasibility:** High. By utilizing a private network, the system eliminates the volatile "gas fees" associated with public blockchains. The software stack is entirely open-source, meaning deployment requires only standard cloud computing resources (Virtual Machines) without expensive licensing fees.
- **Operational Feasibility:** High. Because the system extracts data via a standard REST API, IT departments do not need to pause network operations, deploy new endpoint agents, or undergo massive retraining to integrate CyberQalxan.

# TECHNOLOGY USE

The proposed **CyberQalxan** middleware integrates high-throughput Web2 data storage with Web3 cryptographic verification. The system architecture is designed to operate seamlessly over existing enterprise environments without requiring endpoint modifications.

## 3.1. System Architecture and Operational Phases

The solution's technological foundation is divided into four distinct phases.

### Phase 1: Log Generation & Centralized Storage (Off-Chain)

The system utilizes existing SIEM infrastructure (modeled using Wazuh/OpenSearch). Operating system and application logs are ingested natively by the SIEM. Because OpenSearch utilizes an inverted index optimized for time-series data, our system's subsequent read requests do not interfere with high-speed database write operations, ensuring the SIEM's performance remains unaffected.

### Phase 2: The Aggregator Middleware (The Cryptographic Bridge)

A standalone Python daemon operates as the core processing engine. To account for network latency and late-arriving endpoint logs, it utilizes a strict **"5-Minute Time-Cutoff"** logic. For example, at exactly 12:05:00, the Python script executes a read-only REST API query to the SIEM, requesting all logs timestamped strictly between 12:00:00 and 12:00:59.

Utilizing Python's `hashlib` library, it calculates the SHA-256 hash of each log. SHA-256 is utilized as it is the cryptographic hash standard approved by NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology) for secure digital signatures [4]. Adjacent hashes are concatenated and recursively hashed (`H_parent = SHA256(H_left || H_right)`) to generate a single 64-character Merkle Root. By utilizing a Merkle Tree, the computational complexity of verifying a single log within the batch is reduced to **O(log N)**, making future auditing mathematically highly efficient.

### Phase 3: The Private Blockchain Network (On-Chain Anchoring)

The blockchain layer is built on **Hyperledger Besu**, deployed as a private, permissioned network across 5 isolated Virtual Machines (VMs). It utilizes the **Quorum Byzantine Fault Tolerance (QBFT)** consensus algorithm. Unlike Proof of Work (PoW), QBFT provides **absolute block finality**—meaning a block mathematically cannot be orphaned or forked once minted [5]. The Python Aggregator uses the `web3.py` library to send an HTTP POST transaction to a dedicated Solidity Smart Contract via a JSON-RPC interface. This contract permanently stores the timestamp and its corresponding Merkle Root.

### Phase 4: The Auditor Engine (Automated Verification)

An automated Python script runs as an overnight batch job (e.g., at midnight). To prevent spiking the SIEM's RAM, it sequentially requests logs in 60-second chunks via the REST API. It recalculates the Merkle Tree locally and queries the Smart Contract for the corresponding on-chain root. If a corrupt administrator has altered the SIEM database, the hashes will mismatch, and the Auditor instantly triggers a critical integrity alert.

## 3.2. Software and Hardware Infrastructure

- **Log Forwarding/Storage:** Wazuh Manager / OpenSearch Database
- **Middleware Development:** Python (hashlib, web3.py, requests libraries)
- **Blockchain Protocol:** Hyperledger Besu (Private Permissioned Network)
- **Consensus Algorithm:** Quorum Byzantine Fault Tolerance (QBFT)
- **Smart Contract Language:** Solidity
- **Deployment Environment:** Docker Containers / Linux Virtual Machines

## 3.3. Tests and Analyses to be Performed

To guarantee enterprise readiness, the following rigorous testing protocols will be executed:

1. **Throughput & Load Testing:** The Python Aggregator will be subjected to stress tests to ensure it can retrieve and calculate the Merkle Root for over 600,000 log entries (simulating high enterprise traffic of roughly 300MB per minute) well within the 60-second processing window.
2. **Network Resiliency (Chaos Testing):** To test the QBFT consensus mechanism's fault tolerance, validator nodes will be intentionally taken offline. According to QBFT logic (`N = 3F + 1`), a 5-node network can tolerate 1 completely compromised or offline node while maintaining full consensus. The test will verify that the remaining 4 nodes successfully continue anchoring logs without data loss.
3. **Red Teaming (Penetration & Tamper Testing):** Simulated insider attacks will be conducted where historical logs within the OpenSearch database are manually altered, deleted, or injected with backdated timestamps. The Auditor script will be analyzed to ensure it achieves a 100% detection rate and zero false positives during these simulated breaches.

## 3.4. Technological Feasibility and Risk Analysis

- **Cryptographic Feasibility:** Highly feasible. By utilizing native hashlib C-bindings in Python, hashing hundreds of thousands of strings takes mere seconds, comfortably fitting within the 60-second operational window.
- **Network Risk (CAP Theorem Constraints):** A known technological risk of QBFT is that it prioritizes *Consistency* over *Availability*. If 2 out of the 5 nodes go offline, the network will halt to prevent a chain fork. However, in the context of legal audit trails, halting the system is a preferable security feature compared to recording split/false data.
- **Storage Feasibility:** Because the system only writes one 64-character string per minute to the blockchain, the ledger will grow by less than 10 Megabytes per year, making the long-term node storage requirements exceptionally feasible.

The proposed **CyberQalxan** architecture has massive commercialization potential within the B2B Enterprise cybersecurity market. According to recent industry analyses, the global market for Zero-Trust security architectures is expanding rapidly, as traditional perimeter-based security is no longer sufficient against internal threats [6].

## 4.2. Feasibility and Ease of Integration

A major barrier to adopting new enterprise cybersecurity tools is the operational nightmare of installing new software agents on tens of thousands of employee computers. CyberQalxan solves that problem entirely. The architecture's greatest strength regarding applicability is its **"Zero-Friction Integration."**

- The system requires absolutely zero modifications to existing endpoint agents.
- It interacts exclusively with the central SIEM's standard REST API.

By utilizing standardized Docker containers and Python scripts, any enterprise with a functioning SIEM (such as OpenSearch, Splunk, or IBM QRadar) can deploy this cryptographic verification layer in a matter of hours, making the project highly feasible for rapid real-world deployment.

# SUSTAINABILITY

The sustainability of CyberQalxan is evaluated across three primary dimensions: environmental impact, economic/operational efficiency, and systemic longevity. Unlike traditional Web3 implementations that suffer from massive resource bloat, this architecture is designed to be exceptionally lightweight.

## 6.1. Environmental Sustainability (Green Architecture)

Public blockchains utilizing Proof of Work (PoW) consensus algorithms require massive amounts of electrical energy for cryptographic mining, consuming terawatt-hours of electricity annually [8]. This makes them environmentally unsustainable for daily enterprise IT operations.

Our architecture completely bypasses this issue by utilizing Hyperledger Besu configured as a private network. By employing the Quorum Byzantine Fault Tolerance (QBFT) consensus mechanism, the validator nodes do not compete to solve energy-intensive cryptographic puzzles. They simply verify and sign blocks based on their trusted digital identities. Consequently, the CyberQalxan blockchain network requires no more electrical power or cooling than a standard web server, ensuring a near-zero carbon footprint.

## 6.2. Economic and Operational Sustainability

Enterprise systems generate thousands of logs per second. If a system attempted to store every log directly on a blockchain, the database would bloat to terabytes in a matter of days, resulting in unsustainable cloud storage costs. Our "Off-Chain Merkle Batching" methodology guarantees operational sustainability. By compressing 60 seconds of enterprise traffic into a single 64-character Merkle Root, the system strictly enforces a **1-transaction-per-minute** limit on the blockchain.

- **Predictable Storage:** The blockchain state remains microscopic and grows at a highly predictable, mathematically fixed rate (less than 10 MB per year), regardless of how much log traffic the SIEM processes.
- **Zero Gas Fees:** Because the network is private, the enterprise does not have to purchase cryptocurrency to execute smart contracts, keeping operational budgets permanently fixed.

## 6.3. Systemic Longevity and Risk Mitigation

To ensure the long-term survival of the system against targeted cyberattacks, the architecture incorporates decentralized risk mitigation. The private blockchain relies on 5 distinct validator Virtual Machines (VMs). These nodes will be distributed across physically and geographically isolated network segments (e.g., different cloud availability zones). This isolation ensures that even if a catastrophic network failure or a targeted ransomware attack compromises one data center, the remaining nodes will maintain consensus, ensuring the continuous, uninterrupted anchoring of the audit trail. Furthermore, by utilizing open-source foundational technologies (Python, Hyperledger, Wazuh), the project avoids proprietary vendor lock-in, ensuring long-term software sustainability.
